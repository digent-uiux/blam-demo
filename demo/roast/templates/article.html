How to dial ina pour-overat home

A good pour-over is not a secret recipe. It is one recipe and a habit of changing a single thing at a time until the cup tastes the way the bag says it should.

Why dial in at all

Every lot we roast behaves a little differently. A dense, high-grown washed coffee wants a finer grind and hotter water than a soft natural from lower down. Dialling in is how you meet each bag halfway, and it takes three or four brews, not thirty.

You need a scale, a kettle you can pour slowly from, and a grinder that holds its setting. Everything else is nice to have.

Change one thing per brew. Write it down. That is the whole trick.

Ines Arvo, at the Halden bar

The base recipe

Start here with any of our filter roasts. It is a 1:16.7 ratio, a little strong on purpose, because it is easier to dilute a cup than to rescue a thin one.

Base pour-over

1 cup · cone brewer

Dose
15 g
Water
250 g
Temp
94 °C
Time
2:45–3:15

Grind: medium-fine, like coarse sand. Water: soft, filtered, just off the boil.

Grind first, always

If the brew drains in under two and a half minutes and tastes sharp, go finer. If it stalls past three and a half and tastes dry or bitter, go coarser. Move two clicks at a time and leave the water alone until the grind is right.

The method, step by step

  1. 01

    Rinse the filter with hot water and tip the water out. It warms the brewer and washes out the paper taste.

  2. 02

    Add 15 g of coffee ground like coarse sand, level the bed and zero the scale.

  3. 03

    Bloom with 40 g of water for 40 seconds. Swirl once so every ground is wet.

  4. 04

    Pour to 150 g in slow circles by 1:15, then to 250 g by 1:45. Keep the spout low.

  5. 05

    Give one gentle swirl and let it drain. Aim to finish between 2:45 and 3:15.

Reading the cup

Let it cool for five minutes before you judge it. Sour and thin means under-extracted: finer, or hotter. Bitter, dry and hollow means over-extracted: coarser, or a touch cooler. Sweet and clear with a long finish means you are done, so write the setting on the bag.
